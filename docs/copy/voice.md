# Trickfish voice

## Who we are

We're the friend at the club who went through 902,003 Lichess games and came back with a list. We don't pretend to be grandmasters. We have Stockfish 16, a lot of real games, and no patience for advice that only works against an engine.

**Tagline:** Stockfish finds the best move. We find the one they get wrong.

We're keeping it. It names the tool every reader already uses, puts the opponent ("they") in the frame, and states the product in nine words. The alternatives we tried were either cleverer and vaguer, or turned into a three-part slogan.

## Who we talk to

- **Club and online players rated 1200–1599** who play 1.e4 and want to win more games out of the opening. They know what a fork is, they've heard of the Fried Liver, and they've lost to it at least once. They don't want a repertoire course. They want five minutes of something that works tonight.
- **Coaches** who need ready-made positions and drills for students at that level. They care about the method and the sample sizes, so we show both.

## How we sound

- **Like a player, not a brand.** "Drops a pawn", "the h8 rook is next", "a knight on the rim". Annotation marks (?, ??, !) do the shouting so we don't have to.
- **Short.** A post says one thing. Most sentences are under 15 words, and some are three.
- **Dry, not wacky.** One joke per post at most, and it comes from the position. If the line would work about a toaster, cut it.
- **Generous to the reader, merciless to the move.** We make fun of 7...Ke8, never of the person who played it. We've all played it.
- **Honest about where our numbers stop.** Saying it first is part of the voice: "100 games, small sample."

## Numbers, honestly

1. **Every number comes from `packs/e4e5-white-1200/traps.json`.** If it isn't in the file, we don't say it.
2. **"Scored", never "won".** Score is points per game (win 1, draw ½). 82% is a score, not a win rate.
3. **Say what the percentage is of.** "65% of 2,404 games went wrong here" is a share of games reaching the position, counting every reply that costs 1.2+ pawns. The most common single mistake is a different, smaller number (7...Ke8, 31%). Don't swap them.
4. **Round normally, to whole percents** (64.6 → 65). Don't round up into a nicer phrase: 64.6% isn't "two thirds".
5. **Name the sample when it's small.** If the mistake happened in fewer than about 100 games, say how many (Trap 2: 98%, 48 games).
6. **No promises about the reader's future.** No rating gains, no "win in 10 moves", no time saved. The games already happened. Yours haven't.
7. **The traps are sound, not unbeatable.** When someone finds the best defence you're fine, not winning. Say so.
8. **Don't use the word "every" carelessly.** The positions behind the pack range from 100 to 6,076 games each. "Backed by thousands of games" is true of the pack, not of each trap.
