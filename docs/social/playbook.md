# Social playbook (@trickfishchess)

## Posting times

Two daily slots, both in UTC (November, Europe on CET, US on EST):

| Slot | UTC | Europe (CET) | US East | US West |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "morning" (the question) | 13:00 | 14:00 | 08:00 | 05:00 |
| "evening" (the answer) | 19:00 | 20:00 | 14:00 | 11:00 |

Why: 13:00 UTC is the one hour when Europe is awake after lunch and the US East Coast is starting its day, so the question reaches both. 19:00 UTC is Europe's evening peak and US lunchtime. Six hours is long enough for people to guess and short enough that the answer lands the same day for both. Polls run 300 minutes so they close before the answer. West Coast viewers see the posts later in the day, which is fine for a puzzle.

These are reasoned defaults, not measured. After three weeks, look at the impressions per post by slot and move a slot by an hour or two if one clearly lags. The clocks change in March (and Europe and the US change on different dates), so recheck then. The poster runs twice an hour, so a post can go out up to about 30 minutes after its time, sometimes later when GitHub is busy.

## Replying and engagement

Spend ten minutes a day, in the morning slot after the question and after the answer. Nothing else is needed.

Answer:
- People who answer the puzzle. Right answer: like it and reply with the line and the data share, as in copy.md ("7...Ke6. You'd have been in the 34%."). Wrong answer: like it, no public correction. The evening reply does that for everyone.
- Questions about a line or a number. Reply with the data (game count, score) and say where our numbers stop ("100 games, small sample").
- Someone finding a mistake. Thank them in public, check it, fix the pack and post the fix.
- Coaches asking about the method: answer fully, it is our best sales material. Point them to the method page of the PDF.
- "Is it worth it?" questions: answer plainly, say what it won't do (no rating promises, 1200–1599 only), mention the 30-day refund. One link, no push.

Skip without replying: trolls, bait, "follow for follow", spam, anyone arguing about rating cheats. Mute, don't argue.

Never:
- Mock a player. We mock the move, never the person.
- Promise rating gains, wins or time saved.
- Say "won" when we mean "scored", or swap the 65% of games that went wrong for the most common mistake.
- Invent a number. If it is not in `traps.json`, do not post it.
- Use hashtags, emoji (one a month at most), em dashes, or the banned words in voice.md.
- Drop the link in replies to people who didn't ask. Paste the link only when someone asks where to get it.
- Delete a wrong post silently. Fix it with a reply saying what changed.
- Post by hand in the poster's slots, so the queue and the timeline don't collide.

## Weekly metrics (every Monday, five minutes)

Write them in a spreadsheet, one row per week:
1. Followers gained.
2. Impressions per post, morning vs evening, and the median.
3. Replies and answers to the question posts. The best sign that the puzzles work.
4. Poll votes.
5. Link clicks on Gumroad (Gumroad dashboard or the X post analytics) and Gumroad sales that week.
6. The three best and three worst posts, by format (question, stat, myth, poll, reminder). Do more of what works.
7. Spend on the X API (Developer Console usage). Posts with a link cost far more than posts without one (see setup.md).

## When to post the next pack's teaser

Not before the first pack has had a fair run. Start the teaser when all three hold:
- Day 30 has passed and the queue is empty.
- At least four weeks of metrics exist, so you know what works.
- The next pack's page is in a state where it can be checked against its data, as this one was.

Then: one poll first (Day 25's poll asks which opening) to confirm interest, one "what we found" stat post a week later, and a link only in the reminder slots. Keep the same ratio: question-and-answer posts about the existing pack stay in the schedule, the teaser takes one post a week. Don't announce a date until the PDF exists.
